Evaluating Full-Duplex Speech Dialogue: Landscape & Innovation Opportunities

A research briefing on recent (2023–2026) methods for evaluating full-duplex spoken-dialogue / speech-to-speech (S2S) models — how close they are to real humans, and how to measure turn-taking naturalness — with a ranked shortlist of novel directions for the Mock_AITutor project.

Date: 2026-06-02 Scope: Methods beyond the project's current evaluation stack (Audiobox Aesthetics, DNSMOS, ESPnet "Talking Turns" judge, Full-Duplex-Bench–inspired scalar metrics, LLM-judge rubrics). Method: Multi-source web research with adversarial fact-checking (23 sources fetched, 111 claims extracted, 25 verified by 3-vote adversarial verification → 23 confirmed, 2 refuted).

Bottom line up front

The field has moved past everything in the current stack in roughly the last 12 months, and the move plays directly to this project's strengths (separated per-role audio + per-segment timestamps + word-level forced alignment + a 2× RTX 6000 Ada GPU box). Three takeaways:

  1. Turn-taking evaluation is going multi-turn and task-grounded. Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 → v2 reframes turn-taking as one axis among instruction-following + task competence, scored by an automated spoken examiner.
  2. "Human-likeness" is now measured by Turing-test discrimination — and no S2S system passes; the diagnosed bottleneck is paralinguistic / emotional / persona, not semantics.
  3. The biggest unclaimed gap: nobody compares a model's turn-taking distribution (gaps / overlaps / floor-transfer-offsets) to a human reference distribution with a distance metric, and nobody has used VAP (Voice Activity Projection) as a reference-free turn-taking scorer on full-duplex two-party generated conversations. Both are squarely buildable with the existing infrastructure.

1. Landscape map — grouped by what they measure

A. Turn-taking & overlap behavior (audio-native)

  • Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 [1] — four controlled overlap scenarios: user interruption, user backchannel, user talking to others, background speech. Two timing metrics reported separately: stop latency (overlap onset → model stops) and re-entry / response latency (overlap end → next utterance), plus categorical dialogue behaviors and prosodic adaptation (speaking rate, pitch, intensity, UTMOSv2). Runs on Moshi, Freeze-Omni, Gemini, Nova Sonic, GPT-4o Realtime.
    • Hard number worth citing: GPT-4o Realtime stops fastest (0.18–0.23 s) but re-entry latencies run ~1.16–3.0 s across systems vs. ~200 ms for humans → the best system is 5–6× slower than a human interlocutor.
  • Full-Duplex-Bench v2 [2] (Oct 2025) — multi-turn, with an automated spoken examiner enforcing staged goals under Fast vs. Slow pacing; scores three first-class axes: turn-taking fluency, multi-turn instruction-following, task competence.
  • ESPnet "Talking Turns" judge (Arora et al., ICLR 2025) — already used in this project. Per-chunk turn-taking label agreement.

B. Content / task quality (cascaded or hybrid — transcript-based)

  • URO-Bench [3] — content scored by ASR (Whisper-large-v3 / paraformer-zh) → GPT-4o-mini on accuracy / relevance / clarity / completeness. Its entire speech-side metric set is only 4 metrics: task-accomplish score, UTMOS, WER/CER, a single first-packet latency. Zero turn-taking-dynamics measurement (verified by full-text search). It is a hybrid — GPT-4o-Audio is used only for a small paralinguistic/SRT subset. → This project's pipeline already exceeds it on turn-taking.
  • dGSLM (Generative Spoken Dialogue Language Modeling) [4] — original source of corpus-level IPU / pause / gap / overlap statistics for generated dialogue (the ones already listed in speech_eval/docs/deferred.md).

C. Holistic human-likeness (newest frontier)

  • S2S Turing Test + STuring [5] (ICLR 2026) — 2,968 human judgments, 9 SOTA S2S systems, 28 participants. No system passed (success 0.07–0.31 vs. 0.70–0.87 human-human). Diagnosed via an 18-dimension human-likeness taxonomy → bottleneck is paralinguistic features, emotional expressivity, conversational persona, not semantic understanding. Ships STuring, an interpretable Qwen2.5-Omni-7B-based automatic human-likeness scorer (96–97% discrimination accuracy).
    • ⚠️ Critical caveat: the 9 systems are largely half-duplex / turn-based (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5-Pro, Qwen3, Kimi, Claude Sonnet 4, …) and do NOT include this project's full-duplex backends (Moshi/MoshiVis/PersonaPlex). The "no system passes / paralinguistic bottleneck" finding is about S2S broadly, and the paper self-labels "preliminary." This scope gap is itself an opening.

D. Audio quality (non-interactional)

  • Audiobox Aesthetics, DNSMOS — already used. They say nothing about interaction.

2. Turn-taking naturalness metrics — the math

This is the section most relevant to "naturalness of the turn-taking mechanism," and where the strongest quantitative grounding exists.

The canonical human reference distributions (the comparison target)

  • Modal inter-turn gap ≈ 200 ms; 70–82% of transitions < 500 ms (Levinson & Torreira 2015 [6]; cross-linguistically robust per Stivers et al. 2009, modes 0–200 ms across 10 languages).
  • Switchboard floor-transfer-offset (FTO) statistics (348 conversations, ~38 h) — concrete, ready to use as a reference [6]:
    • Between-overlaps (overlapping transitions): modal 96 ms, median 205 ms, mean 275 ms (75% < 374 ms)
    • Within-overlaps: modal 350 ms, median 389 ms, mean 447 ms
    • 30.1% of floor transfers involve overlap; only 3.8% of total signal is simultaneous speech.

The key methodological move: distribution-to-distribution distance

Best practice is to treat turn-taking as a distribution and score realism by distance to a human reference, not by scalar means:

  • FTO = (start of next speaker) − (end of current speaker); negative = overlap, positive = gap.
  • Build per-system histograms of FTO / gap-duration / overlap-duration / backchannel-timing.
  • Score with Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) distance, 1-D Wasserstein / Earth Mover's Distance (EMD), or Jensen–Shannon (JS) divergence against the human reference.

Verified gap: Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 reports scalar stop/response latency; URO-Bench reports a single first-packet latency. No full-duplex benchmark currently reports a full-distribution distance to a human reference. This is the cleanest open lane.

Predictive turn-taking models as evaluators — VAP

  • Voice Activity Projection (VAP) (Ekstedt & Skantze, KTH) [7] — self-supervised, incremental model on raw stereo waveform + voice activity, outputs a probability distribution over 256 discretized "projection windows" of each speaker's activity over the next 2 s. No annotations, no feature extraction, no speaker normalization. Four turn-taking sub-tasks: Shift-vs-Hold, Shift-Prediction, Backchannel-Prediction, Short-vs-Long.
  • Precedent that matters: the VAP authors already established VAP as an automatic evaluator of turn-hold / turn-yield cues [8] — but only for TTS single utterances, NOT full-duplex two-party generated conversations. Documented risk: VAP scores can be "variable depending on the synthesizer."

Prosodic entrainment (the paralinguistic axis nobody wires in)

  • Kruyt et al. 2023 [9] — 12 distinct entrainment methods (global/local proximity, global/local convergence, local synchrony, LMM, geometric, TAMA, hybrid, cross-recurrence quantification, windowed lagged cross-correlation). Applied to one corpus they give non-identical results — each captures a different entrainment subtype. Method choice is a substantive decision that must be justified, not a detail.

A human-human full-duplex reference corpus that matches this project's data format

  • Zhou et al. 2025 [10] — Chinese 10 h + English 5 h, isolated-room dual-track recording → separate per-speaker tracks, concurrent speech as overlapping intervals, per-line start/end timestamps + speaker ID + transcription. This mirrors the project's tutor_full.wav / student_full.wav + JSONL exactly, making direct distributional comparison feasible. (One refuted overclaim: do not say this design "enables full-duplex modeling rather than turn-based" — that framing was killed in verification.)

3. What's missing / open problems

  1. No distribution-level turn-taking realism metric against a human reference (everyone reports scalars).
  2. VAP has never been applied to full-duplex two-party generated S2S conversations — only TTS single utterances.
  3. Inter-speaker prosodic entrainment is in no full-duplex eval pipeline (not FDB, URO-Bench, Talking Turns, Audiobox, or the Turing-test work) — yet entrainment is the paralinguistic axis the Turing-test paper names as the human-likeness bottleneck.
  4. No reference corpus for task-oriented / asymmetric dyads. Switchboard / Fisher / CANDOR are casual peer chat. A student↔︎tutor interaction is asymmetric and task-driven — their gap/overlap/entrainment distributions may be the wrong target. This domain-mismatch is an open question and a publishable framing.
  5. Confound: turn-taking-mechanism naturalness is entangled with content/pedagogy — a model that monologues looks "turn-taking-poor" for content reasons. Disentangling these is unsolved.
  6. No validity study showing which automatic metrics correlate with human naturalness judgments for true full-duplex dialogue (the Turing-test systems were half-duplex).

4. Ranked innovation directions (tailored to this project's setup)

Ranked by leverage × defensibility × fit to existing infrastructure (separated per-role audio + timestamps + forced alignment + 2× RTX 6000 Ada).

⭐ #1 — Distributional turn-taking realism (highest leverage, lowest risk)

  • What: From the JSONL timestamps, compute FTO / gap / overlap-duration / backchannel-timing distributions per system; score human-likeness as KS / Wasserstein-EMD / JS divergence to a human reference — separating between-overlaps (negative FTO) from within-overlaps and clean gaps.
  • Builds on: Levinson & Torreira / Stivers references; FDB metric definitions. New: no full-duplex benchmark reports distribution-to-human-reference distance.
  • Reference corpus: Switchboard / Fisher (FTO stats already published), CANDOR, or Zhou et al. 2025.
  • Cost: trivial — pure post-processing on data already written.

⭐ #2 — VAP as a reference-free turn-taking naturalness scorer for full-duplex S2S

  • What: Run pretrained VAP over the stereo (tutor/student) audio → score turn-hold/yield appropriateness + predicted shift/backchannel timing on the generated conversation; calibrate against VAP-on-human-human.
  • Builds on: Ekstedt & Skantze's VAP-as-evaluator precedent. New: extending it from TTS single utterances to full-duplex two-party generated conversations — explicitly not yet demonstrated.
  • Reference corpus: any human-human stereo dialogue (Switchboard / Fisher / CANDOR / Zhou).
  • Cost: VAP inference is light on the GPU box. Report VAP-on-human as a calibration baseline (synthesizer-variability risk is documented).

#3 — Bidirectional prosodic entrainment between tutor & student

  • What: Measure prosodic convergence/synchrony (F0, intensity, rate) between the two role tracks using a justified subset of the 12 Kruyt methods (e.g., local synchrony + windowed lagged cross-correlation for dynamics, global convergence for trend); compare magnitude to human-human reference.
  • Builds on: entrainment literature + the Turing-test finding that paralinguistics is the bottleneck. New: entrainment is in no full-duplex eval pipeline. Forced alignment gives clean per-role word/utterance F0 + energy contours.
  • Caveat: must justify which method(s) — they are non-interchangeable.

#4 — Meta-evaluation / validity study (higher effort, high payoff)

  • What: Collect human naturalness/human-likeness judgments on the full-duplex tutoring dialogues (MTurk-style, or the STuring scorer as a target) and test which automatic metric (Audiobox, DNSMOS, ESPnet turn-taking, the new FTO-distance, VAP) actually correlates with them.
  • Builds on: the ICLR-2026 18-dim taxonomy + STuring. New: a validity study for true full-duplex tutoring dialogue — the Turing-test systems were half-duplex and excluded these backends.
  • Cost: needs human data collection; STuring generalization to tutoring is untested.

The strongest single paper here is a fusion: a distribution-level + VAP-based "interactional naturalness" metric suite, applied to task-oriented (tutoring) full-duplex dialogue, with a small human-correlation validity study and a discussion of why casual-chat reference distributions are the wrong target for asymmetric tutoring dyads. That hits four named gaps at once, and existing infrastructure covers ~80% of it.

5. Caveats (read before committing)

  • Very recent / pre-publication: FDB-v2 (Oct 2025) and the S2S Turing test (Feb 2026) are bleeding-edge; the Turing-test paper self-labels "preliminary." Adjacent live work (ParaS2S, MULTI-Bench, HumDial) was noted.
  • Turing-test scope mismatch: its systems are largely half-duplex and exclude this project's backends — apply its conclusions with care (this is also an opening).
  • Reference distributions are corpus/language-dependent, and the Corps et al. (2022, Cognition) critique cautions that phonetic inter-speaker gaps are an imperfect proxy for linguistically-defined turns — relevant if gaps are framed as evidence about speech planning rather than as a descriptive matching target.
  • Two claims were refuted in verification and excluded: that FDB-v1.5 is "the first" automated overlap benchmark, and that the Zhou dual-track design "enables full-duplex modeling." Do not repeat either framing.
  • Directions #1–#4 are synthesis (graded medium/low confidence), not single-source-verified — treat as well-grounded hypotheses, not settled facts.

6. Open questions

  1. Does VAP, trained on human-human telephone/dyadic speech, give meaningful and calibrated turn-taking-cue scores when run on full-duplex S2S model audio (Moshi/MoshiVis/GPT-Realtime), or does the synthesizer-dependent variability noted for TTS waveforms degrade it — and what human-human calibration baseline is needed?
  2. Which automatic metric correlates best with human naturalness/human-likeness judgments for true full-duplex dialogue, and does STuring's discrimination generalize from its training systems to these backends and the tutoring domain?
  3. What is the right human-reference corpus for a tutoring (asymmetric, task-oriented, instructional) dyad? Switchboard/Fisher/CANDOR are casual peer conversation, so their gap/overlap/entrainment distributions may not be the correct target.
  4. How should one disentangle turn-taking-mechanism naturalness from content/pedagogy quality so that distributional gap/overlap/VAP scores are not confounded by the model simply talking more or less?
